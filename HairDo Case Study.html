Use-case story

Meera's Saturday

Meera, 29, wants a honey balayage before a cousin's wedding in three weeks. She has a ₹5,000 budget, one free Saturday and no idea which nearby salon to trust.

Thu 21:10

The trigger

She saves a balayage photo from her feed. Last time she walked into a salon on a hunch and paid ₹6,500 for a colour that faded in a month. She does not want a repeat.

Thu 21:14

Browse by style, not by salon

In HairDo she taps "Honey Balayage" on the home screen. The app already knows the service she means, so she does not need to read five salon menus to find the matching line item.

Thu 21:16

Tell the app what matters

Three sliders: price matters most, stylist skill next, distance least. Five salons are ranked in under a second and one is marked "Best for you" with plain reasons.

Thu 21:20

Compare the close calls

She ticks the top three and opens a side-by-side table. One salon has a 20% colour offer and the lowest price. Another has a 4.8 rating but costs about ₹3,000 more. The table highlights the winner in each row.

Thu 21:26

Pick a stylist and a slot

She opens the winning salon, sees the colourist's 9 years of experience and 4.9 rating, picks Saturday 11:00 and confirms. The offer is applied automatically and the total is shown before she commits.

Sat 13:40

The result

She leaves with the colour she wanted, ₹630 saved through the first-visit offer and a review prompt that asks two questions: did the result match the style photo, and was the price what the app showed?

"I finally compared on the thing I care about, the result, instead of guessing."

Phase 1 and 2 · Idea and discover

Choosing a salon is a trust problem, not a search problem

The idea started from one observation: people choose a hairdo first and a salon second, but every existing way to book works the other way round.

Vision

Help anyone get the hairdo they imagined, at a fair price, from a stylist who has done it well before.

Opportunity

Prices are hidden, offers are scattered across posters and social posts, and stylist skill is invisible until the chair.

Riskiest assumption

People will tell an app what matters to them and trust a ranked answer. Testing this early shaped the priorities sliders.

Insight 1 · Style first

Most interviewees described the look they wanted before naming a service. Browsing by hairdo removes a translation step.

Insight 2 · Price is a surprise

The final bill was often higher than expected because length, colour level and add-ons were not quoted up front.

Insight 3 · The stylist matters more than the salon

People followed a stylist between salons. Skill for this service needs to be visible.

Insight 4 · Offers are hard to compare

A "20% off" and a "₹1,000 off" cannot be compared without maths. The app should show what you actually pay.

The research plan is realistic, but the findings above are illustrative hypotheses for this case study. Validate them with your own interviews before building.

Phase 3 · Define

Who we design for, and what success looks like

Primary persona

Meera, 29 · Product analyst

Colours her hair twice a year, researches before spending over ₹3,000, books on her phone, prefers weekend mornings.

  • Goal: get the look from her saved photo without a surprise bill
  • Frustration: cannot tell who will actually do the work
  • Behaviour: compares two or three options, then books
Secondary persona

Arjun, 34 · Sales manager

Wants a quick, reliable cut near work and rarely changes his style.

  • Goal: fastest good cut within 20 minutes of the office
  • Frustration: unclear waiting times and weekday offers
  • Behaviour: rebooks the same stylist once he finds one

Problem statement

Salon-goers cannot confidently choose where to get a specific hairdo because price, stylist skill and offers are hidden or hard to compare. This leads to overpaying, disappointing results and unused offers.

How might we…

  • …let people start from the hairdo they want?
  • …show the real price after offers before they book?
  • …make stylist skill for this service visible?
  • …turn five salons into one clear recommendation?

Phase 4 · Ideate

What goes into version 1

Features were scored on user value and build effort, then grouped by priority. The five features you asked for are all in the first group.

Must have v1

  • Hairdo gallery with filters for face shape
  • Hair-care treatments with duration, results and upkeep
  • Price comparison by service with offers applied
  • Offers hub across salons
  • Stylist profiles with skills, experience, ratings
  • Smart ranking by your priorities, plus compare
  • Slot booking and confirmation

Should have v1.1

  • Saved styles and favourite stylists
  • Reminders and rescheduling
  • Reviews with before and after photos
  • Payment inside the app

Could have v2

  • Camera try-on for colour and cuts
  • Loyalty points and referral credits
  • Home-service stylists

Not now

  • Product shop
  • Social feed
  • Salon-side inventory tools

Phase 6 · Prototype

Compare salons near you and get the best option

This prototype is fully clickable. The ranking is calculated live from your priorities, offers are applied to each price, and the compare table highlights the best value per row.

9:41HairDo

Try this path

  1. On Explore, tap Honey Balayage.
  2. Open What matters to you and drag Price to the maximum, Distance to 0. Watch the winner change.
  3. Tick three salons and open Compare.
  4. Open the winner, check Stylists and Offers, then book a slot.
  5. Try a hair-care treatment, or the Offers tab.

Salon names, prices, offers and distances are made-up sample data. With your city and a few real salons, this same engine can run on real data.

Shipping the design

Pilot

  • One neighbourhood, 15 to 20 partner salons
  • Salon onboarding: menu, prices, offers, stylist skills

Launch

  • Closed beta with 200 users
  • Public launch in the pilot area
  • Expand one area at a time

How the ranking works

Each salon gets a 0 to 100 match score. Price after offer, distance, rating, best-stylist skill for the service and offer savings are each scaled across the candidate salons. Your sliders weight them. The top score is "Best for you" and its strongest reasons are shown.
